02 / WHY IT MATTERS

A tiny linguistic detail.
A potentially big difference.

“ ”

Language unfolds

Human instructions are not always finished on the first try. Corrections, cancellations and changes of mind are normal conversational practices.

⌁

Transcripts can mislead

A missing sentence boundary or a mistaken verb can disrupt how a downstream system interprets a revised instruction, even when many words are recognized correctly.

◇

Review is not authorization

A linguistic signal can justify pausing for review. It cannot prove that a speaker is authorized to send, delete or change anything.

03 / INITIAL FINDINGS

What did we observe?

In one constructed pilot, 24 scenarios were spoken at two rates, producing 48 recordings. Twelve scenarios contained revisions, giving 24 revision recordings.

The V4 detector performed better on these constructed examples. These are repeated versions of 24 scenarios—not 48 independent linguistic cases.

3/48

Model action choices changed

Whisper transcript differences changed a local text-only Qwen model's symbolic proposal in three paired comparisons—two improvements and one regression.

Text-only model · no direct vocal-authority inference
Research boundary. The findings use researcher-constructed, provisionally labeled English scenarios, synthetic speech and a small local model. No real actions were executed. They do not demonstrate general real-world safety or effects of vocal authority on audio-native AI models.

04 / THE PROJECT

VOXGATE brings
language into AI safety.

WaitWise studies instruction repairs and review flags. VoiceRank studies how speaking rate affects human perceptions of authority, clarity and urgency. Direct audio-model tests are future research.

The full Python/Django research application, speech-recognition pipelines and local Qwen experiments remain on the researcher's computer. This public site offers an intentionally restricted, browser-only port of the text detector.
